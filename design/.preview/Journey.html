The seller
Lands on the page mid-scroll between shows. Wants to know in one screen whether this can answer their chat.
Pastes tonight's link. Ticks “this is my show” if it is theirs. Opens the audio bridge when told what it buys them.

SideStage

Where it breaks
A hero that describes a category. They have seen four of those this week.
Attaching with a catalog that belongs to a different show — every answer abstains and the lineup fact denies real stock.
A confident wrong answer in the first ten minutes. There is no second first impression.
Silence that looks like calm — a 402, a dropped watcher, a queue that stopped filling.
A report that congratulates. If it has nothing to fix, they will not open the next one.
A rung that promotes itself, or one earned on somebody else's shows.
What we watch
paste rate — visits that end with a link in the field
time to first proposal — target under 4 minutes from landing
first-send latency — how long until they trust one enough to send
edit rate and answered rate — the two the PRD lives on
gaps closed — fixes made before the next show
rungs kept — a level turned off after one show has failed

The first ten minutes decide everything

so they are choreographed, not left to chance
00:00
Attached, and it says what it can see

“14 lots seen · 3 named from your speech · price card read from camera.” Not a spinner — a list of what it already knows.

00:40
The first card, with its legend

Six pills explained once, dismissed forever. The card is deliberately an easy one: a shipping question with a policy clause behind it.

02:10
The first abstention

A question it cannot ground appears under “asked · nothing to ground an answer”, not as a card with a Send button. Saying nothing is the feature.

05:30
The first block

A stale price is caught and named, with both versions. This is the moment the product either earns trust or becomes a toy.

09:00
The first action

A markdown proposal with its preflight visible and 90 seconds of undo. Reversible before it is impressive.
