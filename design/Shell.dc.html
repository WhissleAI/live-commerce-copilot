Navigation · v2

A rail, a bar, and a strip that never lets you forget you are live.

Until now each surface invented its own header and the only navigation was an account dropdown. The frame: a 52px icon rail that costs nothing vertically, a 44px content bar, a 34px contextual row, and — while a show is on air — a 28px live strip on every screen that is not the console. Vertical is the scarce axis in a console, so navigation spends width instead.

Inspector I

One panel for every “tell me more” — a reply with its evidence, every verdict and where the time went, or an audit entry and its place in the chain. 380px on a wide screen, Esc to dismiss, never a modal.

1

52px rail, always identical. Seven destinations plus identity, on every screen including the console. It costs 3.6% of the width and none of the height, which is the axis a console cannot spare.

2

44px content bar, 34px sub-nav. The bar names where you are and carries the page\u2019s two actions; the row below switches between the four or five views of that destination.

3

A live strip, everywhere but the console. Open Cost mid-show and you keep the clock, the queue depth and a keystroke back. Losing that awareness was the most dangerous thing about the old design.

4

⌘K navigates and acts; ⌘J asks. The command palette is for going and doing — jump to a show, set autonomy, end a session. Product research keeps its own key, because it is a question, not a command.

Where everything lives

Seven destinations, each with its own contextual row. Nothing is more than two clicks from the rail, and the same thing is never in two places — an action lives in the 44px bar, a view switcher in the 34px row, never both.

At narrow widths
