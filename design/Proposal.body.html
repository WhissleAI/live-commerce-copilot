1

Intent and speech act. The word tells you the topic; the dot tells you whether it was actually a question — filled means answerable, hollow means someone typed “these are gorgeous”.

2

Confidence is reported, not guessed. 0.45 + 0.50 × evidence quality − 0.60 per block − 0.22 per revise. It means the same thing on every card, which is what lets the autonomy ladder threshold on it.

3

Time from the comment landing to this card. Turns red past the 2-second budget — the operator sees the breach, not just the average.

4

The buyer's own words, kept verbatim above the draft, so an edit is made against the question and not against the answer.

5

Provenance, not a citation style. Each chip is the fact the reply is standing on — hover gives the text, the fact id, the retrieval score and the listing version it was read at.

6

Six guards, always in the same order — price · stock · policy · grounding · tone · pii. Fixed positions so the shape is readable at a glance instead of a sentence to parse.

7

“–” is not a failure. It means the guard had nothing to check: a shipping answer makes no price claim. Every guard still runs on every reply, so one block never hides another.

8

One keystroke each. Enter sends, E edits in place, X dismisses, R re-drafts. A blocked card loses its Send button entirely — it is not a warning to click through.

No model is ever asked whether a reply is safe. A guard either points at a fact that contradicts the draft, or it allows — and a guard that throws returns block.
